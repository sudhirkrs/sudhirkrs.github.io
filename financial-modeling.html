Session overview

Learning objectives

By the end, participants can:

  1. Structure a model with separate inputs, calculations and outputs, using standard formatting conventions.
  2. Link the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement so the balance sheet balances by construction.
  3. Build driver-based forecasts for revenue, margins, working capital and capex.
  4. Compute unlevered free cash flow, WACC and terminal value, and arrive at equity value per share.
  5. Run sensitivity and scenario analysis and explain which assumptions drive value.
  6. Cross-check a DCF with trading multiples and audit a model for common errors.

Prerequisites and materials

Basic accounting literacy (what the three statements are) and Excel or Google Sheets. Facilitators need a projector, this page, and a blank workbook. Participants need laptops.

The case company

Kaveri Foods Ltd (fictional): a packaged-foods maker. Last year's revenue is ₹1,000 crore, EBITDA margin 18%, D&A 4% of revenue, capex 5%, net working capital 12% of revenue, tax 25%, net debt ₹300 crore, 50 crore shares. The same numbers feed the live model in section 4.

1. Modeling principles

20 min

A good model is…

  • Simple enough to audit: complexity that nobody can follow is a liability.
  • Flexible: change one input and everything flows.
  • Transparent: one formula per row, no hidden hardcodes.
  • Checked: built-in integrity checks (balance sheet balances, cash ties).

Layout

Separate tabs (or clearly separated blocks) for Assumptions → Calculations → Outputs. Time runs left to right with consistent columns on every sheet. Units and currency labelled in a header.

Color conventions

ColorMeaning
BlueHardcoded inputs and assumptions
BlackFormulas and calculations
GreenLinks to other sheets
RedChecks, errors, warnings
Rules to enforce: never hardcode a number inside a formula; never overwrite a formula with a value; never mix units (₹ crore vs ₹ lakh); keep signs consistent (outflows negative).

Useful shortcuts and functions

F4 anchor refs · Ctrl+[ trace precedents · XLOOKUP/INDEX-MATCH · SUMPRODUCT · NPV / XNPV / IRR / XIRR · Data Tables for sensitivities · IFERROR sparingly (it hides bugs).

2. The three statements and how they link

35 min

The model is only credible if the statements are integrated. Walk participants through this chain on the whiteboard:

  1. Income statement → Revenue, COGS, opex, EBITDA, D&A, EBIT, interest, tax, net income.
  2. Cash flow statement starts from net income, adds back D&A (non-cash), subtracts the increase in working capital, then capex (investing) and debt/equity flows (financing) → change in cash.
  3. Balance sheet: cash = prior cash + change in cash; PP&E = prior + capex − D&A; working capital items from drivers; debt from the debt schedule; retained earnings = prior + net income − dividends.
Assets = Liabilities + Equity → check row must equal 0 every period

3. Driver-based forecasting

25 min

Forecast drivers, not line items. Each driver needs a reason, a source and a range.

LineDriverTypical approach
RevenueVolume × price; or segments; or market shareBottom-up beats flat growth %
COGS / margins% of revenue, with input-cost sensitivityFade to peer margin
OpexFixed + variable splitOperating leverage
Working capitalReceivable, inventory, payable daysDays × Revenue/365
Capex / D&A% of revenue; PP&E roll-forwardCapex ≥ D&A for a growing firm
Debt / interestSchedule with repaymentsRate × opening balance

Forecast hygiene

  • Forecast 5 to 10 explicit years, then a terminal value; fade growth and margins toward sustainable levels.
  • Check outputs against history: does a 25% margin make sense when the best peer earns 20%?
  • Sanity metrics: ROIC, revenue per employee, capex/D&A, cash conversion.
  • Model three cases (base, upside, downside) from one switch cell.

Mini-build (10 min)

In a blank sheet, put revenue growth, EBITDA margin, D&A %, capex % and NWC % in blue input cells for 5 years. Compute revenue, EBITDA, EBIT and ΔNWC with one consistent formula copied across. Then compare with the live model below.

4. Free cash flow, WACC and DCF

40 min

Unlevered free cash flow

UFCF = EBIT × (1 − t) + D&A − Capex − ΔNWC

Cost of capital

Cost of equity (CAPM) = Rf + β × ERP
WACC = E/(D+E) × Ke + D/(D+E) × Kd × (1 − t)

Example: Rf 7%, β 1.0, ERP 6% → Ke 13%. Pre-tax Kd 9%, tax 25% → 6.75% after tax. At 30% debt weight, WACC ≈ 0.7 × 13% + 0.3 × 6.75% ≈ 11.1%. Use target (not current) capital structure, market values, and be consistent with the currency of cash flows.

Terminal value

Gordon growth: TV = UFCFn × (1 + g) / (WACC − g)
Exit multiple: TV = EBITDAn × multiple

Keep g at or below long-run nominal GDP growth (India roughly 8 to 10% nominal, mature economies 2 to 3%); in this exercise 4 to 5% is already generous. Cross-check the implied exit multiple from a Gordon TV.

From enterprise value to price

EV = Σ UFCFt/(1+WACC)t + TV/(1+WACC)n
Equity value = EV − net debt − minorities − pensions + non-operating assets
Value per share = Equity value / diluted shares
Typical pitfall: terminal value often exceeds 60 to 75% of EV. Always report the TV share and treat it as the key judgment, not a plug.

5. Sensitivity and scenarios

20 min

  • Sensitivity: flex one or two variables (WACC × g; growth × margin) using a data table. Identify which input moves value most.
  • Scenarios: a single selector (1 = base, 2 = upside, 3 = downside) driving growth, margin and capex via CHOOSE() or INDEX().
  • Tornado: change each input ±10% and rank the impact on value.
  • Break-even: what growth or margin does the current price imply? (reverse DCF)

Facilitator activity (10 min)

In the live model, ask groups to build the downside case: Y1 growth 6%, Y5 growth 4%, margin 15%. Record the value per share, then the upside (growth 14%/8%, margin 20%). Discuss the range and what would have to be true for each.

Probability-weighting: 25% downside, 50% base, 25% upside gives an expected value; compare it with the base case alone.

6. Trading multiples cross-check

15 min

Process

  1. Pick 5 to 8 genuinely comparable peers (business mix, growth, margins, geography).
  2. Calendarize to the same year-end; use consistent EBITDA definitions.
  3. Compute mean, median, quartiles; use the median to reduce outlier impact.
  4. Apply to the target's metric; subtract net debt for equity value.

Exercise

Peers trade at EV/EBITDA of 12.0×, 13.5×, 14.0×, 15.5×, 18.0×. Kaveri's EBITDA is ₹180 crore, net debt ₹300 crore, 50 crore shares. What is the implied price per share at the median?

AnswerMedian = 14.0×. EV = 14.0 × 180 = ₹2,520 cr. Equity = 2,520 − 300 = ₹2,220 cr. Per share = ₹44.4.

Discussion: the live DCF gives about ₹26.5 per share, versus ₹44.4 from peer multiples. Why might they differ (peer growth and margins, WACC, terminal growth, market sentiment), and which assumption would you challenge first?

7. Auditing and common errors

15 min

Checklist

  • Balance sheet check = 0 in every period; cash on the BS equals cash from the CFS.
  • Formulas consistent across each row (use Ctrl+\ or Go To Special → Row differences).
  • No hardcodes in formula cells; no unexplained plugs.
  • Signs and units consistent; mid-year vs end-year discounting applied the same way to TV.
  • WACC > g, always. TV discounted at year n, not n+1.
  • Net debt uses the same date as the valuation date; diluted share count (options, convertibles).
  • Tax on EBIT (not on EBT) for unlevered cash flows.

Spot the bug (10 min)

Give each pair 3 minutes per case, then reveal.

Case 1: Value per share is ₹900 and TV is 95% of EVTerminal growth set to 9% with WACC 10%, so the Gordon denominator is 1%. Fix: cap g, show TV share, cross-check the implied multiple.
Case 2: Balance sheet is off by exactly the D&AD&A reduced net income but was not added back in CFO (or PP&E was not reduced). Trace D&A through all three statements.
Case 3: UFCF grows 40% but revenue grows 8%ΔNWC sign flipped (an increase in working capital added to cash flow). Check signs on the cash flow statement.
Case 4: EV/EBITDA looks cheap but equity value is negativeEV was used where equity value was needed: net debt was not subtracted (or was added).
Case 5: WACC drops when debt increases without limitCost of debt and beta were held constant. Re-lever beta and raise Kd as leverage rises.

Exercises and capstone

Exercise A: Working capital days (10 min)

Revenue ₹1,200 cr, COGS ₹840 cr. Receivables 45 days of revenue, inventory 60 days of COGS, payables 40 days of COGS. Compute net working capital.

SolutionReceivables = 1,200 × 45/365 = 147.9. Inventory = 840 × 60/365 = 138.1. Payables = 840 × 40/365 = 92.1. NWC = 147.9 + 138.1 − 92.1 = ₹193.9 cr (16.2% of revenue).

Exercise B: WACC (10 min)

Rf 7%, ERP 6%, β 1.2, pre-tax Kd 9.5%, tax 25%, debt/(debt+equity) 25%.

SolutionKe = 7 + 1.2 × 6 = 14.2%. Kd after tax = 9.5 × 0.75 = 7.125%. WACC = 0.75 × 14.2 + 0.25 × 7.125 = 10.65 + 1.78 = 12.43%.

Exercise C: Terminal value (10 min)

Year-5 UFCF = ₹150 cr, WACC 11%, g 4%. Compute TV and its present value.

SolutionTV = 150 × 1.04 / (0.11 − 0.04) = 156 / 0.07 = ₹2,228.6 cr. PV = 2,228.6 / 1.115 = 2,228.6 / 1.6851 = ₹1,322.5 cr.

Capstone (20 min, individual)

  1. Rebuild the Kaveri DCF in a spreadsheet from the inputs above, in your own layout (inputs, calcs, outputs).
  2. Match the live model's value per share within ₹0.50.
  3. Add a scenario selector and a WACC × g data table.
  4. Write three sentences: what the value is, what drives it, and what you would want to verify next.

Assessment rubric: structure and formatting (25%) · correct linkages and checks (25%) · defensible assumptions (25%) · interpretation of sensitivities (25%).

Knowledge check

One-page cheat sheet

UFCF = EBIT(1−t) + D&A − Capex − ΔNWC
Ke = Rf + β·ERP · WACC = wE·Ke + wD·Kd(1−t)
TV = UFCF·(1+g)/(WACC−g) · PV = TV/(1+WACC)^n
EV → Equity: − net debt − minorities − pensions + non-op assets
Per share = Equity / diluted shares
  • Blue = input, black = formula, green = link, red = check.
  • Forecast drivers, not totals. Fade to steady state.
  • WACC > g. Terminal value is a judgment, show its share of EV.
  • Triangulate: DCF + multiples + sanity ratios (ROIC, margins vs peers).
  • Always report a range, not a point.
